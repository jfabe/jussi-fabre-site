About

A seafarer who designs the networks

Ship cybersecurity usually arrives from one of two directions: IT people who have never stood a watch, or crews handed rules written for an office. This practice sits between them — STCW A-II/1 Officer of the Watch service on RoRo and cargo vessels (Viking Cinderella, Finnstar, Finnfellow, Finnsky, Finnmerchant, Finntide, Finnbreeze, Hjördis), and a quarter century of carrier-grade, industrial and cloud network architecture.

Watch duty during a periodic RINA classification survey at a shipyard in southern Sweden gave first-hand exposure to how class inspects hull, machinery, fire safety and dry-docking. Combined with a Bachelor of Maritime Management (master-mariner programme, 270 ECTS) from Novia University of Applied Sciences, that is the foundation for newbuilding, quality and class-facing work.

Advisory · Ship networks & OT security

From class requirement to a network that sails

Since IACS UR E26 and E27 became mandatory for ships contracted from 1 July 2024, cyber resilience is a class condition, not an IT wish-list. Owners, yards and integrators have to show that propulsion, DP, bridge and alarm networks are segmented, documented and maintainable — without slipping the delivery date.

01 · Assess

Where does the vessel or fleet stand today?

  • Gap analysis against UR E26 / E27 and IEC 62443
  • OT asset inventory and zone & conduit model
  • Vessel-to-shore and vendor remote-access risk review
  • Prioritised findings, sized for budget and dry-dock windows

Outcome: a clear, costed picture for management and class.

02 · Design

What should the network look like?

  • Reference architecture — segmentation, redundant rings, QoS, firewalled conduits
  • Vendor-neutral selection of type-approved switches, routers and IEC 62443-4-2 components
  • Connectivity strategy — 4G / 5G / 450 MHz, satellite, WLAN
  • Secure remote-access model for service partners

Outcome: a specification yards and suppliers can quote against.

03 · Deliver & verify

How does it reach approval — and stay compliant?

  • Owner's representative between yard, suppliers and class
  • Supplier E27 documentation review, FAT / SAT witnessing
  • Class survey preparation and finding closure
  • Crew handover, change management and lifecycle patching plan

Outcome: approval on schedule and a vessel that stays compliant.

The added value
